Evolving NCAs with Epiplexity

Epiplexity (Finzi et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.03220) captures the structural information present to a computationally bounded observer, excluding noise that is otherwise signal to an unbounded observer under Kolmogorov complexity.

We evolve NCAs toward increased learnable complexity through an epiplexity heuristic with a fixed linear probe that acts as a bounded student. To prevent evolutionary search settling in collapse basins (rollouts that settle to a constant or low-variance field), a gzip band on the rollout is multiplied into the fitness as a filter.

Probe ladder — different students

The runs above use a fixed linear probe. Here we sweep the student — the bounded observer whose prequential length defines the epiplexity signal — across a complexity ladder, all at p_drop = 0 (no cell dropout). A heavier student can compress more structure, so a given rollout looks less complex to it; the evolved NCAs differ accordingly.

Each card: the 1000-step no-dropout rollout of the best-ever individual, its final achieved gzip ratio and prequential length. The three groups are the probe-training horizon modes — direct (one forward pass to state t+K), autoregressive (probe applied K times), and multi (loss summed across horizons 1,2,4,8,16).
